# REGEN-045 — Civilization Continuity Bridge v1

Status: preregistration only.

This contract defines how local and regional regenerative-service outcomes may be composed into larger continuity analysis without allowing a resilient site, neighborhood, settlement, municipality, region or single service to stand in for “civilizational resilience.”

It consumes REGEN-040 essential services, REGEN-041 dependency closure, REGEN-042 compound shocks, REGEN-043 qualified substitutions and REGEN-044 repair/skill recovery state.

## 1. Core theorem

```text
bounded local/regional service outcomes
+ explicit population and geographic scopes
+ inter-scope dependency graph
+ mutual-aid/resource-flow constraints
+ temporal horizon
+ service-criticality profile
+ explicit unresolved state
= inspectable continuity composition
```

not:

```text
one resilient community
= resilient region
= resilient nation
= civilization continuity
```

## 2. Scale is explicit

Every continuity statement must bind an exact scale/scope such as:

- household/site;
- facility;
- neighborhood;
- settlement/community;
- municipality;
- regional network;
- larger federation/territory.

The model must not silently upgrade one scope to another.

## 3. Population denominator is explicit

A continuity outcome must bind the population or service-recipient denominator it concerns.

```text
service maintained for some recipients
!= service maintained for the full declared population
```

Unserved or displaced demand remains visible.

## 4. Service portfolio is plural

Civilizational continuity is not one service.

The bridge should permit adopted profiles referencing services such as:

- potable/usable water;
- food/nutritional service;
- sanitation/waste handling;
- essential energy;
- shelter/building habitability;
- communications;
- health-supporting logistics/infrastructure;
- mobility/transport where declared essential;
- repair/maintenance capacity;
- seed/propagation capacity;
- soil/ecological function;
- information/coordination capability.

REGEN-045 does not invent universal minima for these services.

## 5. Service criticality is adopted policy, not model truth

Which service is essential, at what minimum, for which population and for how long is an explicit adopted profile.

Symthaea may analyze consequences of different profiles but cannot create the profile's normative authority.

## 6. Local pass does not aggregate by counting passes

A region with ten “passing” settlements may still have a shared upstream vulnerability.

The bridge must compose transitive dependencies rather than count green nodes.

```text
N local PASS labels
!= regional PASS
```

## 7. Local failure does not imply larger-scale failure

Regional storage, mutual aid, diversified imports, spare capacity, relocation of service delivery or external support may absorb local failures.

The model must therefore avoid both localist optimism and local-failure catastrophism.

## 8. Inter-settlement flows are first-class

Continuity may depend on flows of:

- food;
- water;
- energy/fuel;
- spare parts;
- equipment;
- people/skills;
- laboratory/testing capacity;
- transport capacity;
- data/communications;
- finance/payment capability;
- waste/residual handling;
- seed/planting material.

Each flow must preserve source, destination, capacity, timing and dependencies where modeled.

## 9. Mutual aid is not infinite

A mutual-aid source can only contribute resources or capacity that remain available after its own needs and commitments.

```text
neighbor has reserve
!= reserve available to every neighbor simultaneously
```

Competing demand remains explicit.

## 10. Exporting fragility is visible

One locality may preserve its own service by drawing down another locality's reserve or ecological capacity.

The bridge must not report that as system-level resilience without showing the transferred burden.

## 11. Shared failure domains cross jurisdiction boundaries

Administrative separation does not imply physical independence.

Multiple municipalities/regions may share:

- watershed;
- transmission corridor;
- fuel depot;
- port/rail corridor;
- cloud/telecom provider;
- upstream supplier;
- laboratory;
- repair specialist;
- payment/finance rail;
- governance/authorization service.

These common modes remain visible across scopes.

## 12. Geographic proximity is not dependency identity

Nearby communities may be independent for one dependency and highly correlated for another.

Likewise distant communities may share one global upstream dependency.

Distance alone does not establish independence.

## 13. Demand transfer is explicit

Failure in one location may increase demand elsewhere through migration, evacuation, market substitution, service transfer or mutual aid.

The continuity model should permit demand to move rather than keeping each locality's denominator artificially fixed when the declared scenario includes transfer.

## 14. Demand transfer does not imply a migration prediction

Synthetic transfer scenarios are scenario assumptions, not forecasts of actual human movement.

The model must distinguish declared demand-transfer fixtures from observed demographic evidence.

## 15. Capacity follows the receiving context

A receiving region with nominal spare capacity may still face constraints in storage, transport, staffing, water, sanitation, shelter, grid capacity or other service dependencies.

```text
spare nominal capacity
!= absorbable transferred demand
```

## 16. Temporal horizons remain plural

Continuity should distinguish at least:

- immediate continuity;
- short-duration bridging;
- medium-duration degraded operation;
- recovery;
- long-duration adaptation/reconstruction.

A system may fail one horizon and recover in another.

## 17. Graceful degradation is first-class

Continuity is not only PASS/FAIL.

The model should represent service trajectories such as:

```text
Normal
DegradedButMeetingFloor
BelowDeclaredFloor
Unavailable
Recovering
Recovered
Unresolved
```

Exact names may change during implementation.

## 18. Minimum floor and delivered service are distinct

The bridge should preserve both:

- declared minimum requirement;
- observed/simulated delivered service.

This prevents “barely above floor” from appearing identical to abundant service.

## 19. Duration below floor matters

A short interruption and sustained failure may have different consequences.

The model should preserve deficit duration and magnitude where the service contract supports them, without inventing universal harm weights.

## 20. Recovery does not erase prior deficit

Later recovery must not rewrite history.

```text
recovered at tN
!= service was continuous from t0..tN
```

Adverse intervals remain evidence.

## 21. Irreversible or path-dependent outcomes are explicit

Some losses may not be fully repaired by later service restoration within the model horizon.

Where an authoritative domain models such path dependence, the bridge may carry that outcome; it must not assume every deficit is reversible.

## 22. Stocks deplete across scales

Regional reserves, local reserves and emergency stocks must preserve exact commitments and depletion.

A reserve moved from region A to region B is no longer simultaneously available in A unless explicitly replenished.

## 23. No double counting of flexible capacity

A single flexible asset or capacity pool cannot simultaneously cover multiple mutually exclusive contingencies in the same realized campaign state.

Examples include:

- mobile generators;
- transport fleet;
- repair crews;
- laboratory capacity;
- stored food/water;
- financial reserves;
- communication equipment;
- temporary storage.

## 24. Trade is part of continuity, not the opposite of it

External trade, regional exchange and diversified supply may materially increase continuity.

REGEN-045 therefore rejects:

```text
more local = automatically more civilizationally resilient
```

The relevant question is whether the composed dependency graph remains capable under the declared shock set.

## 25. Local capability remains valuable when it adds optionality

Local production/repair/storage can improve continuity where it:

- removes a consequential single point of failure;
- adds an independent service path;
- shortens recovery time;
- provides bridge capacity;
- preserves essential knowledge/skill;
- reduces correlated dependence;
- enables qualified substitution.

These are testable propositions rather than ideological defaults.

## 26. Ecological floors remain hard constraints at every scale

Larger-scale continuity may not be manufactured by aggregating local ecological overdraw.

A plan that maintains human service by violating adopted ecological obligations must preserve that violation rather than converting it into a resilience success.

## 27. Rights/authority remain scoped

Mutual aid, regional transfer or emergency coordination does not itself create property, access, withdrawal, professional, governance or physical-operation authority.

The bridge references the authoritative state; it does not mint authority.

## 28. Quality and safety remain service-specific

A resource moved across scopes must still meet the relevant quality/safety proposition for its intended service use.

Quantity transfer cannot manufacture suitability.

## 29. Infrastructure state and institutional state are distinct

A physical resource path may exist while coordination, finance, contracting, identity, credential or authorization systems fail.

Conversely, institutional permission cannot create unavailable physical capacity.

Both remain explicit.

## 30. Information/communications continuity is a dependency

Coordination across multiple scopes may require communications and trustworthy state sharing.

The bridge should expose this dependency rather than assume perfect global knowledge during shocks.

## 31. Local operation must survive central information loss where designed to

Where a service is intentionally locally autonomous, loss of federation/central coordination should not be modeled as physical loss of the local service unless the actual local dependency graph requires it.

This preserves the wider Mycelix principle that coordination failure should not unnecessarily terminate essential local operation.

## 32. Evidence lineage remains scale-bound

Local outcome evidence must not be generalized to a larger scope without explicit composition.

A region-level outcome should identify the local/regional evidence and model state it consumed.

## 33. Observation, simulation and recommendation remain distinct

The bridge may consume:

- observed outcomes;
- deterministic synthetic outcomes;
- model projections;
- recommended interventions.

These classes must not be collapsed.

```text
simulated continuity
!= observed continuity
```

## 34. No collapse-probability claim from synthetic campaigns

Synthetic scenario failures do not establish real societal-collapse probability or election/political forecasts.

REGEN-045 is a systems-assurance bridge, not a geopolitical prediction engine.

## 35. No universal civilization score

The output remains plural.

A continuity representation may include, for each service/scope/horizon:

- requirement state;
- delivered-service trajectory;
- deficit magnitude/duration;
- dependency closure;
- substitution state;
- recovery state;
- population served/unserved;
- unresolved evidence;
- ecological/right/safety status;
- common-mode dependencies.

The core assigns no universal weighted score.

## 36. Cross-service coupling is explicit

Services may depend on each other.

Examples:

```text
water -> energy
energy -> communications
communications -> coordination
transport -> food/spares
repair -> energy/water/storage
```

Cycles must be represented rather than flattened into independent service rows.

## 37. Service-restoration ordering matters

When repair/resources are scarce, restoring one service first may accelerate or delay others.

The model may compare declared restoration strategies but does not invent governance priority.

## 38. Critical-service profile is versioned

The adopted set of services/floors/horizons should have exact identity/version references.

A continuity result is valid only relative to that profile.

## 39. State revisions require re-evaluation

Changes to population, stocks, infrastructure, service requirements, authority, ecological state, shock state or dependency availability invalidate ambient reuse of an old continuity result.

## 40. Mutual-aid agreements and actual deliverability are distinct

A standing agreement may create coordination/authority context but does not prove the source has capacity during the realized shock.

Likewise available capacity without an agreement does not automatically create legitimate transfer authority.

## 41. Recovery capability is not preparedness proof

A network capable of eventually recovering from a failure may still experience unacceptable service deficits during the horizon.

Recovery and continuity remain separate outcomes.

## 42. Redundancy quality is structural

The model should distinguish:

- duplicated assets with shared upstream failure;
- genuinely independent alternatives;
- geographically diverse but supplier-common alternatives;
- local + regional + external layered redundancy;
- cold standby vs immediately usable capability.

Counting assets alone is insufficient.

## 43. Knowledge/skill continuity is a service dependency

Where continuity depends on rare skills or tacit knowledge, REGEN-044 state must compose into the larger continuity graph.

A region with many assets but one qualified maintainer remains concentrated.

## 44. Regenerative knowledge may itself be continuity infrastructure

Seed propagation knowledge, soil-management evidence, water stewardship knowledge, repair skills and contextual recipe lineage can be treated as dependencies where declared.

This does not make every stored document actionable or valid in every context.

## 45. Terra-Preta proving-family boundary

The first proving family may ask whether a community soil loop improves larger continuity by preserving soil/crop service under selected shocks.

The bridge must still expose dependence on:

- feedstock/ecological retention;
- water;
- energy/process capacity;
- equipment/spares/skill;
- testing/quality;
- storage/logistics;
- seed/crop systems;
- rights/authority;
- regional mutual aid and external supply.

A local amendment loop is therefore one node in continuity composition, not “civilization resilience” by itself.

## 46. Symthaea boundary

Symthaea may:

- compose service/dependency graphs;
- simulate shock/recovery trajectories;
- identify bottlenecks/common modes;
- compare preparedness or investment candidates;
- estimate uncertainty;
- propose experiments and resilience improvements.

Symthaea may not:

- define normative service minima without adopted authority;
- create emergency authority;
- waive ecological/safety/rights constraints;
- convert simulations into observed claims;
- execute physical interventions.

## 47. Mycelix boundary

Mycelix may coordinate authoritative identities, evidence, service requirements, commitments, mutual-aid agreements and cross-domain state.

REGEN-045 does not become a central command authority over local services.

## 48. Initial deterministic model direction

A later dependency-light model may define structures conceptually equivalent to:

```text
ContinuityScope
PopulationScopeRef
ServicePortfolioRef
ServiceTrajectory
InterScopeFlow
DemandTransfer
MutualAidCommitmentRef
ContinuitySnapshot
ContinuityCompositionOutcome
```

using exact references to domain-owned state.

The pure composition layer should not import device-control, networking, Holochain persistence or learned policy logic.

## 49. Initial evaluation order

A deterministic composition pass may use a stable sequence such as:

```text
scope/population binding
-> adopted service profile
-> local service trajectories
-> cross-service dependencies
-> inter-scope flows/commitments
-> demand transfer
-> shared failure domains
-> substitutions/repair state
-> hard ecology/rights/safety gates
-> resource contention/double-counting checks
-> horizon-specific continuity outcomes
```

## 50. Minimum synthetic campaign

A first executable campaign should include at least:

1. one local service PASS does not create regional PASS;
2. multiple local PASS nodes sharing one failed upstream dependency expose regional failure;
3. local failure is absorbed by qualified regional capacity;
4. mutual-aid capacity cannot be spent twice;
5. transfer preserves depletion at source;
6. receiving capacity limits absorbed demand;
7. demand transfer changes service denominator explicitly;
8. short local outage followed by recovery retains outage history;
9. service below floor for part of horizon remains explicit;
10. one service recovery depends on another service restoration;
11. restoration ordering changes scenario trajectory;
12. geographically separated suppliers with same upstream source expose common mode;
13. diversified external trade improves continuity where qualified;
14. local reserve improves bridge duration without implying indefinite closure;
15. regional reserve exhaustion creates later deficit;
16. ecological violation cannot become continuity PASS;
17. unresolved rights cannot become legitimate transfer;
18. quantity-sufficient but quality-failed transfer remains unusable;
19. communications failure removes coordination path but not independent local operation where no physical dependency exists;
20. central-system failure does not automatically stop locally autonomous service;
21. observed local outcome and simulated regional outcome remain distinguishable;
22. synthetic campaign frequency is not emitted as collapse probability;
23. stale population/service profile invalidates prior outcome;
24. skill concentration from REGEN-044 remains visible at regional scale;
25. one mobile asset cannot cover two simultaneous exclusive deployments;
26. recovery after the declared continuity horizon remains recovery, not continuity;
27. service abundance above minimum is distinguishable from barely meeting floor;
28. unresolved evidence prevents false certainty.

## 51. Relationship to REGEN-046

REGEN-045 defines the composition machinery and scale bridge.

REGEN-046 should define the plural outcome vector used to report continuity without collapsing it into a universal score.

## 52. Relationship to REGEN-047

REGEN-047 should adversarially attack continuity composition for:

- hidden common modes;
- fake mutual-aid capacity;
- reserve double counting;
- correlated regional shocks;
- communication/coordination assumptions;
- skill concentration;
- stale evidence;
- scale-generalization errors.

## 53. Deliberate non-claims

REGEN-045 creates no current claim of:

- national or civilizational resilience;
- disaster readiness;
- societal-collapse probability;
- political stability;
- adequacy of any real emergency plan;
- sufficiency of any real food/water/energy reserve;
- emergency authority;
- legitimacy of resource transfer;
- ecological sufficiency;
- physical action.

Its proposition is deliberately narrow:

> larger-scale continuity can only be inferred through explicit composition of bounded service outcomes, populations, interdependencies, mutual-aid/resource flows, time horizons and hard constraints; local resilience claims never upscale automatically.
