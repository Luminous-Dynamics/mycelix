# Hospitality Outcome Measurement Protocol v0.1

Status: **proposed field-measurement protocol; read-only and non-authorizing**

## 1. Purpose

A demand model cannot be credibly evaluated against an “actual” that is missing, selectively recorded, self-reported with unknown bias, or itself generated by a model. This protocol therefore separates food-waste measurement quality from forecast quality and from causal business impact.

It is designed to work alongside:

- `HOSPITALITY_FIELD_PILOT_RUNBOOK_V0_1.md`;
- `HOSPITALITY_SOURCE_EXPORT_COMPLETENESS_CONTRACT_V0_1.md`; and
- `crates/mycelix-business-waste-measurement`.

The new Rust crate validates a preregistered measurement denominator, method-specific evidence, timestamps, calibration validity windows, per-slot outcome accounting, coverage thresholds, safe integer arithmetic, and a deterministic evidence digest. It is a structural contract—not proof that a physical act occurred.

## 2. Why this extra layer is necessary

A February 2026 open-access study of food-waste tracking systems in healthcare and hotel kitchens reports that staff-entered food-waste tracking can materially under-report waste: around 30% in the reported aggregate, and up to 80% during lunch service in one healthcare kitchen. The paper also reports strong relationships between coverage/weighing frequency and reporting bias (`R² > 0.80`). These estimates belong to the studied settings; they must not be presented as universal restaurant under-reporting rates. Their relevance is that reporting coverage and measurement method can dominate apparent model improvements.

Source: “How accurate are food waste tracking systems? Insights from healthcare and hotel kitchens,” *Resources, Conservation & Recycling*, February 2026. <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S092134492500566X>

A September 2026 preprint presents a restaurant food-waste prediction dataset whose target is derived from an explicit formula with controlled stochastic variability because large-scale ground-truth waste measurements are scarce. That is useful for reproducibility and early method comparison; its reported metrics are against a constructed target, not a measured-food-waste ground truth. Never use a synthetic/imputed target as if it were a direct physical observation.

Source: “A Machine Learning Framework for Predicting Restaurant Food Waste to Support Sustainable Food Management,” arXiv, 8 September 2026. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08078>

## 3. Five distinct outcome states

Every planned measurement slot must have exactly one explicit disposition:

1. **Direct weighing** — a net mass derived from gross mass minus tare, with scale identity and a calibration-evidence reference valid at the observation time.
2. **Count-derived** — counted units multiplied using a frozen conversion rule or recipe digest.
3. **Visual estimate** — an observed amount estimated through a frozen method; not equivalent to direct weighing.
4. **Model-imputed** — a predicted or statistically imputed quantity; retained for operations analysis but never silently treated as a physical observation.
5. **Not observed** — the planned slot remained unmeasured and a reason is recorded.

These states are not interchangeable. A slot populated by a model is not thereby a measured slot. A direct-weighing record with a calibration digest is not thereby authenticated or proof that a scale was used.

## 4. Freeze the protocol before the measurement window

Register and retain digests for:

- the restaurant/site scope, source identity, and half-open UTC interval;
- the versioned waste-category and disposition definitions;
- the planned slot IDs and slot/sampling plan;
- the threshold policy for missing and imputed slots;
- the minimum direct-weighing share;
- the mass unit and rounding rules;
- allowed instrument/calibration evidence policy;
- the method and conversion-rule versions;
- the data adapter and mapping versions; and
- the exact candidate forecast/recommendation lineages, when those outputs will later be compared.

Registration must predate the measurement window. In the executable contract, every slot ID is bound to an exact target timestamp, allowed observation window, and waste category. Physical observations must fall inside the slot window; same-category slot windows may not overlap; and a missing outcome cannot be declared until its slot window closes. If a semantic input changes, start a new evidence lineage rather than reclassifying previous observations.

Slots should be defined at a useful operational grain (for example site × service period × waste category) with exact predicates and non-overlapping interpretation. The operator must choose this grain before seeing candidate model errors. Avoid sample plans that over-represent quiet periods while missing busy shifts.

## 5. Sampling, collection, and audit

- **Keep the denominator visible.** Every planned slot must be represented by a measurement record or an explicit not-observed record. Missing slots cannot disappear during CSV cleanup.
- **Use independently selected audit slots.** Preregister a reproducible random or otherwise defensible sampling method. Retain selection inputs and digests; do not replace difficult or inconvenient selections after observing outcomes.
- **Blind physical measurement where practical.** Staff weighing waste should not be shown Symthaea's prediction for that slot until the measurement has been recorded, where operations allow. This reduces (but does not eliminate) anchoring and expectancy effects.
- **Log the full method.** Retain target time, observed time where applicable, recorded time, site/category/reason, net mass or derivation, record-evidence digest, and any instrument/conversion evidence.
- **Check instruments.** Record the scale identity and calibration-validity interval. Authentication of calibration evidence and instrument suitability are external checks; a non-zero digest alone proves neither.
- **Audit observer/process effects.** Repeat a preregistered subset with an independent observer or instrument where feasible. Report the disagreement distribution and missingness by daypart, waste class, and service conditions.
- **Never confuse waste categories.** Freeze whether the measure includes prep trimmings, spoilage, unsold prepared food, returned plate waste, liquids, packaging, bones, or inedible portions. Preserve disaggregated categories rather than changing definitions after results are seen.

The protocol's Rust validator is not a substitute for the physical measurement process. It can validate the consistency of submitted evidence while still being unable to know whether the submitted measurements represent all waste generated. Model-imputed outcomes have a target time but must not invent a physical observation timestamp; directly weighed, count-derived, and visual observations must record a physical observation time within the exact preregistered slot window.

## 6. Conservative metrics and gates

For `N` preregistered slots, let `M` be explicitly not-observed slots, `I` be model-imputed slots, and `W` be directly weighed slots.

- Missing-slot rate: `M / N`.
- Imputed-slot rate: `I / N`.
- Direct-weighing coverage: `W / N`.
- Non-direct observed estimates are counted separately; they do not increase direct-weighing coverage.
- Report mass from direct weighing, count-derived estimates, visual estimates, and model imputation separately. Do not add them into one headline “measured waste” total without a clear label.

Rates must use conservative integer basis-point arithmetic in the verifier: upper limits round upward and minimum coverage rounds downward. The v0.1 protocol has explicit, preregistered limits for missing and imputed slots and a minimum direct-weighing share. The numeric values should be selected for the actual pilot design and registered before collection; they are not claimed as universal restaurant standards.

An unaccounted slot, duplicated slot, duplicate measurement identity, out-of-window physical observation, invalid mass arithmetic, invalid calibration interval, or missing required evidence must fail closed. A missing outcome must be recorded explicitly, never inferred from a shorter list.

## 7. Model-evaluation rules

1. Compare demand forecasts with source data whose completeness/semantics have been qualified separately.
2. Evaluate waste-reduction hypotheses against the strongest available independently measured outcomes, with direct-weighing share and measurement coverage reported prominently.
3. Exclude model-imputed values from the physical-ground-truth denominator. If using them for development, label the experiment as imputation/simulation-only.
4. Compare candidate and baseline forecasts on the same fixed target set and temporal split; prevent future-data leakage.
5. Keep the baseline simple and preregistered (for example same-weekday/daypart seasonal-naive) before testing more elaborate models.
6. Measure business results separately from forecast accuracy: absolute waste mass, stockouts, service level, cost, sales volume, menu mix, and operating conditions.
7. Do not claim causal savings from a shadow evaluation or a before/after comparison alone. Use a controlled or matched-location design when feasible, report confounders, and calculate net benefit after measurement, integration, staff, hardware, and operating costs.

Forecast error can be low against a biased label; observed waste can fall because demand fell; measured waste can appear to rise because the process began capturing more. These alternatives must remain visible in the final evaluation.

## 8. Privacy, safety, and authority

- Use site- and role-level identifiers. Avoid storing named employee identifiers when a pseudonymous role/observer reference is sufficient.
- Retain only operational data needed for the registered measurement purpose.
- Protect raw records and calibration documents with normal access, retention, and audit controls.
- This protocol does not authorize Symthaea to change purchasing, staffing, pricing, payments, or stock records.
- Any future automation must be separately qualified through the Business authority, Action Contract, decision, coordination, revalidation, receipt, and reconciliation stack.

## 9. Evidence states and wording

Allowed wording should name the scope and evidence, for example:

> Under preregistered protocol X, Y of N planned hospitality waste-measurement slots were directly weighed, Z were estimated, I were model-imputed, and M were unobserved. Instrument/calibration and observer-audit coverage are reported separately. The result is limited to the registered site, period, waste categories, and sampling design.

Do not claim “ground truth,” “complete measurement,” “waste reduced by X%,” or “verified savings” unless independent evidence actually supports that exact statement.

## 10. Acceptance criteria for a field pilot

Before using the pilot to assert a waste-reduction advantage, require:

- preregistration before data collection;
- a complete slot denominator with explicit missingness;
- qualified source export and measurement method lineages;
- sufficient direct-weighing coverage under preregistered thresholds;
- valid calibration evidence for the required instruments;
- reported disagreement and coverage by relevant operational slices;
- candidate/baseline comparisons on the same cases;
- independently reviewed outcome data; and
- a separate, appropriately designed estimate of causal and net economic impact.

This v0.1 artifact improves the rigor of measurement records and claims. It does not itself establish that Symthaea predicts waste better than existing software or that any restaurant will save money.
