# SOV-DPI-000 — Sovereign DPI Adoption Constitution v1

Status: draft / normative architecture proposal

Issue: #1025

## Purpose

Mycelix should be deployable by jurisdictions with radically different administrative, economic, and political structures without allowing deployment customization to silently weaken the protocol-level constitutional boundaries that make the substrate trustworthy.

The governing theorem is:

`national customization != constitutional degradation`

This document defines the adoption boundary only. It does not create public authority, certify any jurisdiction, or establish production readiness.

## Composition rule

SOV-DPI MUST compose existing Mycelix authority/evidence work rather than introduce a parallel government authority stack. Relevant predecessors include AC-001/#870, reciprocal accountability/#28, institutional-core/#31, sovereign interoperability/#489, GOVSYS-001/#783, capital/evidence architecture/#883, and AGENT-000/#798.

## Sovereign policy space

A deployment MAY vary, subject to local law and separately qualified policy:

- administrative topology and institutional structure;
- centralized, federal, municipal, cooperative, or mixed operating models;
- taxation, benefits, procurement, industrial, and service-delivery policy;
- data residency, retention, language, and localization policy;
- federation membership and cross-border interoperability policy;
- approved infrastructure operators and providers;
- service composition and migration sequencing.

None of those choices may by themselves mint protocol authority or disable the invariant floor below.

## Constitutional invariant floor

### SDPI-001 — customization is not authority

A jurisdiction/profile declaration is configuration evidence. It cannot create a new authority source by declaration alone.

### SDPI-002 — centralized administration is not unbounded authority

Mycelix may represent strongly centralized institutions. Centralization does not remove exact subject, scope, purpose, provenance, currentness, delegation, challenge, or expiry requirements where those requirements apply.

### SDPI-003 — observation is not coercive authority

Model output, reputation, wealth, stake, analytics, anomaly scores, predictions, or capture signals cannot directly create coercive/public authority or directly remove fundamental civic standing.

### SDPI-004 — emergency authority cannot self-expand

Emergency authority must have an independently legitimate source, bounded scope/consequence, explicit start/expiry semantics, reviewability, and no self-extension or self-expansion path.

### SDPI-005 — person-linked access is accountable

Person-linked access must remain purpose/scope/authority bound and preserve required accountability evidence. Cross-domain composability is not blanket permission to join person-linked data.

### SDPI-006 — disconnection cannot increase authority

Loss of network, verifier freshness, revocation visibility, or upstream evidence can preserve only explicitly precommitted bounded autonomy. It cannot increase authority.

### SDPI-007 — foreign evidence is not local sovereign authority

Evidence may cross sovereign/federation boundaries. It cannot directly mint local authority without local qualification under the receiving authority constitution.

### SDPI-008 — capital is not constitutional authority

Funding, infrastructure ownership, provider market share, compute ownership, token balance, or creditor status cannot by themselves mint public/constitutional authority.

### SDPI-009 — vendor dependency is not sovereignty

A deployment advertised as sovereign must have a defined path to continued operation, inspection, recovery, export, and provider replacement without dependence on Luminous Dynamics as a mandatory control plane.

### SDPI-010 — low-resource operation preserves the same floor

Lower-resource profiles may reduce throughput, optional features, or service availability. They must not silently weaken authority, accountability, challenge, provenance, privacy, or anti-capture invariants.

### SDPI-011 — consequential public decisions remain contestable where policy requires contestability

Challenge, appeal, review, correction, and remedy artifacts must remain representable in the institutional substrate. An adapter/profile may not erase them merely for implementation convenience.

### SDPI-012 — AI is not sovereign by intelligence

AI/model systems may advise, propose, analyze, simulate, rank, or exercise separately granted narrow operational capabilities. Intelligence, confidence, consciousness-related measurements, or model identity do not create governmental authority.

## Threat census

SOV-DPI-000 treats at least these as first-class adoption threats:

1. foreign vendor/control-plane dependency;
2. domestic executive authority widening;
3. ministry/agency privilege creep;
4. permanent emergency authority;
5. model/advisory output promoted into public authority;
6. cross-domain person-linked surveillance joins;
7. removal of challenge/appeal/remedy paths;
8. provider lock-in or non-exportable public state;
9. low-resource exclusion hidden behind deployment assumptions;
10. partition/offline operation widening authority;
11. corrupted registry/procurement/evidence inputs;
12. donor or development-finance capture;
13. capital ownership becoming protocol authority;
14. foreign evidence becoming local authority without local qualification;
15. disappearance or compromise of Luminous Dynamics.

## Adoption theorem

The target property is not political uniformity. It is:

`local sovereignty + interoperable evidence + bounded authority + provider replaceability + common invariant floor`

A jurisdiction can therefore use Mycelix without becoming subordinate to another jurisdiction, Luminous Dynamics, an infrastructure vendor, a validator cartel, or an AI system.
