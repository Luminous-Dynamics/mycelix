# CSCP Statement-to-Cash Reconciliation Contract

**Status:** research contract paired with an in-memory Rust reference model in draft PR #5172.
**Design principle:** reported earning, payment obligation, and observed cash are different facts.
**Research checked:** 2026-10-18.

## Executive decision

Do not let a royalty statement silently become cash received. Creator economics has at least three evidence classes:

1. **Reported revenue / payable assertion** — a distributor, licensing society, label or platform says that a line earned or is payable.
2. **Settlement expectation** — a statement, invoice, contract or deal says what should be paid, when, and under what terms.
3. **Observed cash** — a distinct transaction record shows that money entered the creator's account or a designated payout account.

These can be connected, but should never be collapsed into a single "income" event. A statement can be revised, delayed, offset, disputed, netted against fees, paid in partial instalments, combined with other statements, or not paid at all.

## Standards evidence and boundaries

The standards landscape itself supports keeping layers distinct:

- [DDEX Recording Data and Rights (RDR)](https://ddex.net/standards/recording-data-and-rights/) separates RDR-N notification of recordings/contributors/rights claims from RDR-R revenue reporting for resource usage. DDEX's current implementation guidance identifies RDR-N 1.5 and notes that implementation requires the applicable DDEX implementation licence. Do not treat a rights metadata payload as a cash transaction or claim conformance merely because field names look similar.
- [DDEX Sales and Usage Reporting (DSR)](https://ddex.ddex.net/standards/sales-and-usage-reporting/) reports sales, usage and revenue generated by services to rights owners for onward royalty processing. That is evidence about usage/revenue, not by itself proof that an end-recipient's bank balance increased.
- [Open Banking API specifications](https://standards.openbanking.org.uk/api-specifications/latest/) describe consented ways to access account information. Transaction identifiers and remittance references can support matching where the source provides them, but the application must preserve source, account scope, transaction identity and missing-reference uncertainty rather than invent a match.
- [ISO 4217](https://www.iso.org/cms/%20render/live/en/sites/isoorg/home/standards/popular-standards/iso-4217--currency-codes.html) includes currency codes and minor-unit relationships. A three-letter syntactic check is not registry validation, and not all currencies use two decimal places. Before human-facing import/export is released, use a dated/versioned ISO 4217 precision table rather than hard-coding cents. This follow-on is tracked in [CSCP-CURRENCY-001](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/mycelix/issues/5193).

## Data model v1

The normalization boundary is deliberate: preserving a source's semantics is a prerequisite to financial reconciliation, not optional metadata. A raw usage/revenue report must not be re-labelled as payable or cash without an explicit mapping contract.


### RoyaltyStatementLine

A statement line in this prototype is a **normalized payable assertion**: the source has been interpreted, by an explicit versioned mapping, as saying a positive amount is payable for this line and reporting period. It records a stable line ID, statement ID, reporting-source ID, reporting interval, currency, and integer minor-unit amount.

Do not map every external field named revenue or royalty directly into this type. DDEX RDR-R and DSR can report revenue generated from usage or sales/usage data, which is not automatically the net amount payable to this specific creator. A future adapter must retain raw source semantics and declare whether a field is usage revenue, reported/accrued royalty, payable amount, or actual remittance; it may create this normalized payable line only when a versioned mapping and supporting evidence justify that interpretation. RDR-N recording/right/party metadata is yet another distinct class of source fact.

A normalized payable line does not update the cash ledger, establish legal entitlement, create an invoice, or prove payout. Missing statement identifiers are not fabricated. V1 models positive payable assertions; signed adjustments, withholding/tax, and platform-fee treatment require explicit typed event kinds in a later schema rather than being encoded as ambiguous negative cash.

### Observed receipt

Cash evidence is an existing creator-ledger MoneyEvent whose kind is Receipt, with source event ID, source reference, time, currency, and positive minor-unit amount. A fee, operating cost, refund, invoice, or royalty statement is not a cash receipt.

This reference model uses the caller's ledger as its receipt source; it does not connect to a bank or payout API and does not verify an export's signature/authenticity.

### Approved allocation

An allocation explicitly connects one statement line to one observed receipt for a positive amount, with an allocation ID, the receipt event ID, creator/reviewer ID, and evidence reference. Multiple allocations may split one statement line across instalments; one actual receipt may settle portions of multiple statement lines. Aggregate allocations may never exceed either line amount or receipt amount, and currencies must match exactly.

An equal amount and currency are only a candidate match—not proof. There is no auto-allocation on amount equality alone. A source reference may inform a future candidate ranker, but any future ranker must offer a reviewable suggestion rather than committing a financial match without an explicit approval action.

### Allocation reversal

A correction does not erase the prior allocation. A reversal event identifies the allocation it cancels and records approver, reason and evidence reference. A reversed allocation releases the affected capacity, so a replacement match can be added, while original and reversal remain visible. An allocation may be reversed only once; exact replay of the same reversal is idempotent.

This is an in-memory append-only model, not a durable event store. Persisting allocations and reversals must be transactionally linked to the ledger receipt and provenance manifest before it can be relied on in production.

## Core invariants

- A statement line never changes observed-cash totals.
- Only a ledger Receipt event can be the receipt side of an allocation.
- The statement line and cash receipt must have the same currency; there is no implicit FX conversion.
- Each source statement-line ID and allocation ID is idempotent for identical content and fails closed if reused with different content.
- Active allocations for each statement line are at most the reported amount.
- Active allocations for each cash receipt are at most the observed receipt amount.
- Failed allocation/reversal attempts leave the book unchanged.
- Reversal preserves the original allocation and records its own approval/evidence.
- Unmatched and partially matched amounts remain visible as outstanding; the system must not show "paid" based on statement-only evidence.
- An allocation records who approved it and an evidence reference, but the reference implementation does not authenticate the approver or prove the source evidence is true.
- A currency code's syntax is not proof of registry assignment or minor-unit precision.

## Release benchmarks — targets, not results

| Benchmark | Target |
|---|---:|
| Statement-to-cash separation | 100% of statement-only fixtures leave observed cash unchanged |
| False automatic matches | Zero; amount equality alone never commits an allocation |
| Allocation ceiling | 100% rejection of allocations exceeding a statement line or cash receipt |
| Currency mismatch | 100% rejected; no implicit FX |
| Incremental settlement | Exact minor-unit allocation and outstanding remainder under arbitrary valid partial payments |
| Duplicate import/allocation | Identical replay idempotent; conflicting reuse of same ID rejected |
| Reversal correctness | 100% reversal/replay fixtures preserve history and release capacity once |
| Failure atomicity | 100% failed operations leave the book byte/logically unchanged |
| Multi-statement payout | Exact sum of allocations never exceeds receipt; remaining cash stays unallocated |
| Unmatched liability | 100% statement-only obligations remain visibly outstanding, never reported as received cash |
| Auditability | Every allocation has approver and evidence reference; 100% reversal entries have reason/approver/evidence |
| Privacy | Zero cross-creator leakage of raw statements or account transaction references |
| Currency precision before external display | 100% codes validated against a dated ISO 4217 dataset and exponent used by its version |
| Suggested matching (future) | Candidate ranking evaluated on adjudicated fixtures; zero silent auto-commits; false-positive and abstention rates separately reported |
| Durability (future) | Allocation, reversal, provenance receipt and canonical ledger mutation commit atomically; zero duplicates under crash/concurrency tests |

Do not interpret targets as measured. Publish exact-head source, toolchain, command, raw output, fixture digest and exit status for every verified gate. CI must fail closed if a test is skipped or its exact subject is not pinned.

## Adversarial fixtures to keep forever

1. A statement line and bank receipt happen to have the same amount and currency, but no source reference or review evidence exists. Expected: unmatched.
2. One statement of USD 100.00 is paid in USD 40.00 and USD 60.00 instalments. Expected: exact partial then full match.
3. One USD 100.00 receipt is intended to settle two lines of USD 70.00 each. Expected: refuse allocation that would exceed the receipt.
4. A EUR statement and USD receipt happen to have numerically identical minor-unit amounts. Expected: reject; never convert implicitly.
5. A fee event has the same amount as the statement line. Expected: reject as a receipt.
6. A duplicate statement line is re-imported unchanged. Expected: idempotent; no second statement line.
7. A line ID is reused with another statement ID or amount. Expected: conflict, not overwrite.
8. A correct allocation is later discovered to be wrong. Expected: reversal event, visible history, and capacity released once.
9. A reversal is replayed or another reversal targets the same allocation. Expected: identical replay is idempotent; a second distinct reversal is rejected.
10. A rejected or incomplete import leaves the statement/cash book unchanged.
11. The statement uses a currency code whose ISO precision is not two decimals. Expected: internal integer units retained; display/parser uses the versioned currency-precision registry.
12. A payout nets earned revenue against fees/withholding. Expected: do not force the gross statement amount to equal the net receipt; typed fee/withholding/correction model remains separate until an explicit bridge is implemented.
