# LARP-RESTORATION — Ecological Outcome and Stewardship Contract v2.0

**Status:** research contract profile; no live restoration project or ecological outcome verified  
**Lineage:** historical Mycelix-DeSci ProofOfRestoration Pact v1.0

## 1. Purpose

This contract qualifies institutional commitments to restore or steward an ecosystem over time. It describes the local mandate, affected parties, baseline, target trajectory, monitoring methods, outcomes, externalities, staged finance, challenges and remedies.

It is not a new ecological definition or certification. Ecological success must be defined with domain experts, affected communities and current standards appropriate to the ecosystem and intervention.

## 2. Current domain references

Compose with existing standards and methodologies:

- **SER International Principles and Standards for the Practice of Ecological Restoration, third edition (June 2026):** assessment, planning, implementation, ongoing management and monitoring/evaluation; updated Restorative Continuum and Five-star System. This is the primary broad ecological-restoration reference. https://www.ser.org/general/custom.asp?page=serstandards
- **IUCN Global Standard for Nature-based Solutions, second edition (launched 10 October 2025):** the current IUCN page lists eight criteria and 27 indicators for NbS design and assessment. Use where the intervention is a Nature-based Solution; not every restoration action is necessarily an NbS. https://iucn.org/our-work/topic/iucn-global-standard-nature-based-solutions
- **Verra VM0047 v1.1:** active ARR methodology for carbon-removal accounting, using remote sensing and plot-based sampling with a dynamic performance benchmark for additionality/baseline within its scope. It is not a complete biodiversity, hydrology, community-rights or ecosystem-integrity measure. https://verra.org/methodologies/vm0047-afforestation-reforestation-and-revegetation-v1-1/

LARP's contribution is contract, authority, evidence, dependency, remedy and finance assurance across the lifecycle—not replacement of those domain methods.

## 3. Local mandate and rights

Before activation, the contract identifies:
- bioregion, ecosystem type and spatial boundary;
- land/resource/operational authority and exact local constitutional basis;
- interested and affected parties;
- applicable Indigenous Peoples' rights and Free, Prior and Informed Consent requirements where relevant;
- locally adopted ecological reference ecosystem and restoration objective;
- prohibited activities and rights floor;
- grievance, challenge, appeal and remedy route;
- access, stewardship, data sovereignty and exit terms.

A high canopy or carbon result cannot cure missing land authority, consent or rights protections.

## 4. Baseline and counterfactual

Pin:
- baseline date and observation window;
- reference sites or comparison units;
- historical and current land-use context;
- climate and disturbance context;
- measurement methods and versions;
- uncertainty and missing-data rules;
- additionality and leakage assumptions where relevant;
- how background temporal/spatial variation is separated from intervention effects.

A before/after difference alone is not a causal impact estimate. Use an appropriate reference or comparison design, repeated observations, and qualitative/local knowledge where scientifically and ethically appropriate.

## 5. Multi-dimensional outcome vector

Do not make one HDC similarity or NDVI value the definition of restoration.

Define a local outcome vector as applicable:
- native species composition and regeneration;
- biodiversity and habitat connectivity;
- structural complexity and biomass;
- soil function and contamination;
- water quality, soil moisture and hydrological flows;
- coastal/marine or inland-water indicators where relevant;
- acoustic ecology or species activity only where validated methods exist;
- carbon stocks/removals where a valid methodology applies;
- ecosystem services and resilience;
- distributional and affected-party outcomes.

For every dimension specify target range, minimum floor, method, calibration, uncertainty, observation frequency, source owner, independence requirement, and conditions under which it is not measurable.

## 6. Externalities and common-cause assurance

Define impact boundaries beyond the funded site:
- upstream and downstream flows;
- adjacent habitat and migration corridors;
- displaced land use or leakage;
- water/energy/resource extraction;
- supply-chain and maintenance dependencies;
- cumulative and cross-project impacts.

Different sensors or organizations are not automatically independent. Preserve known shared providers, data lineage, funding and control dependencies. Incomplete inventory is indeterminate, not proof of independence.

## 7. Monitoring and evidence lifecycle

Evidence records bind:
- observation location and coverage;
- method/instrument/model version;
- calibration and uncertainty;
- source owner and collection party;
- effective, observation, receipt and knowledge times;
- provenance and transformations;
- dependency graph and inventory completeness;
- consent, access, retention and disclosure limits;
- revocation, correction and supersession.

Freshness is specific to the feature and method. A stale observation can remain valid historical evidence while being ineligible for a present-day release decision. Do not apply one universal exponential half-life to every ecological variable.

## 8. Staged finance

The contract can prepare a bounded tranche request when locally defined outcomes and evidence conditions are met.

Each tranche binds:
- contract and constitution digests;
- bioregion and steward entitlement;
- exact recipient, tranche amount, remaining/total ceiling, currency and funding source;
- tranche number and schedule;
- evidence snapshot and independent assessment;
- externality and rights checks;
- stop/resume conditions;
- appeal and correction route;
- logical-operation identity and expiry.

The treasury owner performs its own current authority, balance, recipient, exact-operation and idempotency checks. LARP does not transfer funds. Provider acknowledgement is not proof of settlement; payment must be independently observed and reconciled.

## 9. Pause, challenge and remedies

Pause or hold when:
- required monitoring is unavailable;
- baseline/method version cannot be reconciled;
- outcome is partial or unknown;
- a minimum ecological floor is breached;
- externality limits are exceeded;
- local mandate, consent or rights become disputed;
- evidence independence is incomplete;
- stewardship or maintenance obligations are not covered.

An anomaly score alone is not fraud. Severe allegations require independent evidence, notice, conflict-screened review, reasons and appeal. Remedies may include monitoring extension, corrective work, revised targets, restitution or prospective termination. History is never deleted.

## 10. Contract lifecycle

```text
MandateAndConsent
  → BaselinePinned
  → PlanRatified
  → ActiveMonitoring
  → InterimAssessment
  → (TrancheRequest | Hold | Challenge | Amendment)
  → IndependentObservation
  → Reconciliation
  → Renewal | Exit | Termination | Archive
```

A changed method, baseline or target creates a versioned amendment with explicit local ratification. An old approval is not silently carried forward into a new constitution or measurement method.

## 11. Benchmark programme

The companion scenario corpus covers:
- fully qualified positive tranche;
- canopy gain with biodiversity collapse;
- downstream water harm;
- shared sensor dependencies;
- incomplete provider inventory;
- stale measurements;
- unbridged method change;
- disputed land mandate/consent;
- anomaly-only fraud accusation;
- partial ecological observation;
- steward exit and continuity;
- ecological success with no treasury authorization.

Report separately:
- ecological outcome by dimension;
- baseline/counterfactual validity;
- uncertainty and coverage;
- externality threshold violations;
- affected-party rights and distribution;
- dependence/independence qualification;
- financial release accuracy;
- administrative and monitoring cost;
- challenge and remedy outcomes.

Compare conventional milestones, relevant domain-methodology baselines (SER/IUCN/VM0047 where applicable), and LARP staged contracts on matched sites or synthetic cases. Do not compare a carbon-credit metric to a complete ecosystem-health profile.

## 12. Validation status

This profile and its synthetic scenarios are research artifacts. No actual ecosystem restoration result, independent field observation, carbon removal, certification or financial transfer is claimed.
