# LARP v2 — HEART Participant Outcome Profile v0.1

**Status:** research integration profile; no telemetry implementation or field study claimed  
**Reference:** Rodden, Hutchinson and Fu, *Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale*, CHI 2010.

## 1. Purpose

HEART is an optional participant-experience measurement method for institutions operating under LARP contracts. It is not an authority model, constitutional legitimacy score, security control, legal standard or substitute for qualitative participation.

The framework's five dimensions are:
- Happiness
- Engagement
- Adoption
- Retention
- Task success

The original HEART method connects product goals to signals and measurable metrics. LARP adopts that measurement discipline while adding safeguards for civic, workplace, research and commons settings.

Reference: https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/

## 2. Institutional adaptation

| HEART dimension | Possible institutional question | Required counter-metrics |
|---|---|---|
| Happiness | Do participants experience dignity, procedural fairness, trust and felt safety? | fear of retaliation, perceived coercion, distrust, distress, unaddressed complaints |
| Engagement | Can people participate meaningfully and voluntarily? | burden per contribution, unwanted exposure, compulsory attendance, unequal speaking time |
| Adoption | Can intended participants access and understand the service? | exclusion, accessibility failures, language barriers, failed onboarding, digital divide |
| Retention | Do people choose to continue because the institution remains useful and trustworthy? | exit friction, lock-in, loss of benefits on exit, coercive dependency, inability to export records |
| Task success | Can participants accomplish the task they came to perform? | failed appeals, uncompleted remedies, repeated submissions, abandonment after confusing processes |

A higher score is not always better:
- high retention can be evidence of lock-in;
- high engagement can reflect conflict, harassment or compulsory participation;
- high adoption can hide unequal outcomes;
- task success can be achieved through denial of due process;
- reported happiness can be distorted by fear or power asymmetry.

## 3. Measurement contract

Each HEART measure must declare:
1. institutional goal;
2. participant population and eligibility definition;
3. dimension;
4. signal and metric formula;
5. numerator, denominator and exclusion rules;
6. observation window and sampling cadence;
7. collection method and instrument version;
8. consent basis and purpose limitation;
9. language/accessibility design;
10. disaggregation dimensions and suppression thresholds;
11. counter-metrics and known confounders;
12. data owner, retention schedule and deletion/withdrawal behavior;
13. confidence/uncertainty and missingness;
14. contract obligation to which the measure is relevant, if any.

A measure without its denominator, sampling rule and missingness report is not comparable.

## 4. Privacy, consent and safety invariants

- Participation in surveys or experience instrumentation must be voluntary unless a clearly defined legal or operational requirement applies; any such exception must be locally justified and independently reviewed.
- Never infer consent from service use, employment, membership, benefit receipt or silence.
- Collect the minimum data needed. Prefer aggregated, privacy-preserving outputs over person-level experience profiles.
- Do not expose raw free-text survey responses to supervisors, funders or reputation systems by default.
- Do not make access to housing, healthcare, livelihood, education, participation or appeal conditional on HEART participation or a score.
- Do not use individual HEART measures for punishment, ranking, hiring, promotion, removal or credibility discounts.
- Suppress small cohorts where participants could be reidentified.
- Publish collection purpose, retention, access, correction and withdrawal routes.
- Measure missing responses and non-response bias; do not silently treat missingness as satisfaction.
- Preserve subgroup analysis only where lawful, necessary, safe and sufficiently aggregated.

## 5. Rights and outcomes remain separate

HEART is descriptive evidence about experience. It cannot establish that an institution is legitimate, constitutional, safe or compliant.

The following remain non-compensatory:
- rights-floor compliance;
- consent and lawful basis;
- ability to challenge decisions;
- remedy availability and timeliness;
- ability to exit without retaliation;
- accessibility and non-discrimination;
- privacy and data minimization.

A rights violation cannot be offset by a high Happiness or Task-success score.

## 6. Benchmark plan

A HEART pilot should pre-register:
- hypotheses and target groups;
- instruments and question wording;
- comparison cohort or baseline;
- sample-size reasoning;
- observation windows;
- disaggregation and suppression rules;
- missing-data policy;
- qualitative interview method;
- counter-metrics;
- stopping conditions for harm.

Report distributions, response rates, subgroup coverage, confidence intervals and qualitative themes. Do not publish one composite HEART score.

Prospective goals:
- improve voluntary task completion and participant understanding;
- reduce challenge and remedy completion time;
- reduce accessibility and language gaps;
- reduce exit friction;
- improve perceived dignity and procedural fairness;
- achieve these without worsening coercion, retaliation, privacy, exclusion or rights outcomes.

## 7. Integration boundary

HEART observations can become evidence artifacts consumed by LARP obligations and LISE assurance reviews. The measurement service does not:
- grant authority;
- amend a contract;
- determine who is credible;
- authorize or deny a rights-bearing decision;
- execute a remedy;
- override a participant's rights.

An institution may decide that a HEART observation warrants investigation or contract review, but any resulting action must use the institution's local authority, evidence, notice, challenge and remedy process.

## 8. Validation status

This profile defines a proposed measurement boundary. No survey instrument, field study, participant cohort, HEART implementation or empirical improvement is claimed.
