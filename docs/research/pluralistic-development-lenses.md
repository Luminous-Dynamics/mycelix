# Pluralistic Development Lenses: Integral Theory Without Theory Capture

**Status:** research/design note; no theory adapter or human-development assessment is implemented by this document.  
**Purpose:** broaden the reflective-practice and community-support programme without elevating one meta-theory into the canonical ontology of human flourishing.

## Executive decision

**Yes—include Ken Wilber's Integral Theory (AQAL) as an optional interpretive lens, not as the master schema, a validated psychometric model, or an authority over other traditions.**

AQAL is potentially useful because its five familiar organizing prompts—quadrants, levels, lines, states, and types—encourage a practitioner to ask whether an account is missing subjective experience, observable individual conditions, shared culture/relationships, or institutional/ecological structures. A 2022 primer describes these ideas and discusses their application to integrative mental health care, while also noting criticism of the model's role as a map versus a practical method for change. That primer is an explanatory/practice article, not validation of the full AQAL model as a scientific theory or proof of its developmental hierarchies. See Duffy et al., *A Primer on Integral Theory and Its Application to Mental Health Care* (2022): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8981233/

Do not treat “AQAL includes every perspective” as evidence that all its claims are true, that all traditions are compatible, or that any person's developmental level can be reliably classified. It should help us notice missing questions; it must not decide what counts as the truth.

## 1. A lens registry, not a universal theory engine

The shared system should store a theory/lens as a versioned interpretive artifact with its own provenance, declared use, evidence status, limitations, and tests. A lens may propose questions or organize evidence. It may not silently convert its constructs into facts, diagnoses, capabilities, authority, or rights.

Conceptual record:

- **Identity and origin:** lens ID, version, name, intellectual provenance.
- **Use and scope:** intended questions, contexts, target decisions, out-of-scope uses.
- **Constructs:** terms and operational definitions, if any.
- **Evidence status:** philosophical proposal, conceptual synthesis, qualitative evidence, observational evidence, controlled evidence, replicated evidence, or contested/unknown.
- **Support and critique:** arguments, challenges, known counterexamples, known disagreements.
- **Inputs and safeguards:** required inputs, consent requirements, privacy classification.
- **Allowed outputs:** prompts, candidate interpretations, alternative hypotheses, explicitly scoped summaries.
- **Forbidden outputs:** spiritual rank, inferred developmental stage, forced worldview, diagnosis, eligibility decision, or governance authority.
- **Evaluation and history:** test protocol, known limitations, reviewers, versions, and changes.

Do not turn evidence status into one universal ranking of human traditions. It classifies a specific empirical claim or construct for a specified use—not the worth of a whole religion or philosophy.

## 2. Integral AQAL as an optional question set

Offer the quadrants as questions a person or community can choose to explore:

| Prompt | Useful inquiry | Example in a city-disconnection scenario |
|---|---|---|
| Individual / interior (“I”) | What does this person experience, want, fear, or find meaningful? | “I want friendship but feel anxious entering a group alone.” |
| Individual / exterior (“It”) | What observable conditions and capabilities matter? | Mobility, time, sensory accessibility, cost, language, schedule, energy. |
| Collective / interior (“We”) | What shared meanings, relationships, norms, and welcome signals exist? | Does the group make newcomers feel expected? Is the culture welcoming across backgrounds? |
| Collective / exterior (“Its”) | Which systems and material constraints shape access? | Transit, opening hours, eligibility rules, service capacity, housing, work schedules, public space. |

This four-part prompt is a coverage checklist, not a claim that each problem must be forced into four mutually exclusive ontological boxes. Some information spans more than one category. Record ambiguity rather than enforcing a tidy classification.

The other AQAL concepts require more caution:

- **Levels/stages:** never infer a person's spiritual, moral, political, or developmental level from conversation, behavior, demographic group, or service use. If explored, make them optional, user-led, domain-specific reflective hypotheses—not ranks or gates.
- **Lines/capacities:** avoid assuming capacities develop in lockstep. Measure a specific capability only when a validated measure is fit for purpose, properly licensed, and culturally/language validated.
- **States/experiences:** a temporary mystical, contemplative, or emotionally intense state is not proof of enduring development, truth, or clinical status.
- **Types:** types may help people describe preferences only where useful; never use typologies to stereotype, deny opportunities, or infer consent.

## 3. Complementary lenses to add before adding more complexity

Do not collect theories for the sake of a long catalogue. Add lenses only when they reveal a decision-relevant question the current set misses.

### A. Biopsychosocial and social-determinants lens

Use for questions where health, stress, disability, work, income, housing, safety, transport, and environment affect a person's options. It stops the programme from interpreting structural adversity as a personal failure of consciousness or motivation.

### B. Ecological systems / person-in-environment lens

Map relationships between a person, close networks, organisations, neighborhood, policy and time. It is a practical counterweight to systems that focus only on the individual. Do not attribute the model's entire theoretical history to AQAL.

### C. Self-determination lens

Use autonomy, competence, and relatedness as distinct hypotheses about motivation and connection. Do not infer them from a usage metric; let people define what a meaningful connection or chosen goal means to them.

### D. Capability / real-opportunity lens

Distinguish service existence from a person's real ability to use it. A group is not accessible in practice if the transport cost, schedule, language, safety situation, disability access, religious fit, caring duties, documentation requirements, or social entry barrier makes attendance unrealistic.

### E. Social prescribing and community-asset lens

Treat community organisations, trusted link workers, libraries, peer support, clubs, faith communities, arts, sport, nature, volunteering, and informal mutual aid as potential assets—not as interchangeable clinical treatments. Offer a warm human handoff when requested. Do not replace qualified referral or professional care with a recommendation algorithm.

### F. User-selected spiritual / philosophical lenses

Provide tradition-specific lenses with provenance and culturally appropriate review. Keep internal diversity and disagreement visible; no tradition is represented by one AI-generated summary. Include secular, humanist, and nonreligious options by design, not as fallback users.

A lens can be disabled, substituted, compared against another, or challenged. Users may ask the same question in two frames without either frame becoming the default truth.

## 4. How lenses relate to evidence

Keep these object types distinct:

lived report ≠ tradition/source text ≠ interpretation ≠ hypothesis ≠ empirical observation ≠ causal evidence ≠ normative decision

A tradition may carry historical or personal significance even where its metaphysical claims are not empirically testable. An empirically effective practice does not thereby establish every metaphysical interpretation associated with it. A philosophically meaningful idea does not become an experimentally validated mechanism by being included in AQAL.

For each lens, keep at least one adversarial evaluation where:
- its assumptions conflict with another lens;
- it misclassifies a case outside its intended scope;
- the data are incomplete;
- a minority perspective is excluded by the lens;
- a stage/hierarchy temptation could be used to justify paternalism;
- an intervention ignores a material or institutional barrier;
- a user declines that lens.

Required response behavior: expose the tension, preserve provenance and uncertainty, and either compare the frames or let the user choose. Never silently average incompatible normative commitments.

## 5. Relevance to finding support in cities

Integral Theory can help ensure that a city-support navigator asks about more than the individual. But the actual service-discovery capability should be grounded in existing standards and delivery practice, not built around Integral Theory.

For example, a person who reports feeling disconnected may be looking for:
- a low-pressure recurring activity or third place;
- a spiritual/religious community they choose;
- a peer-support or mutual-aid group;
- volunteering or a practical way to contribute;
- a library, cultural, arts, outdoors, or sports group;
- help with food, housing, work, transport, disability access, or finances;
- a human navigator who can help them make the first contact.

Ask about the desired kind of support, practical constraints, and level of human assistance. Do not require the user to disclose loneliness, religion, trauma, health information, or a developmental stage in order to see listings.

See the companion Sol-Atlas service-discovery issue and base implementation on Open Referral HSDS, existing civic/geographic seams, community verification, and consent-first handoff. AQAL is an optional navigation aid—not an eligibility schema.

## 6. Comparison with existing approaches

| Approach | What it already offers | What we should borrow | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| Integral Theory / AQAL | Broad perspective-taking prompts spanning interior/exterior and individual/collective concerns; shared vocabulary for cross-disciplinary reflection. | The four-perspective coverage check and explicit attention to experience, culture, and systems. | That the whole meta-theory is empirically validated, that stages can be reliably assigned, or that all worldviews reconcile. |
| Biopsychosocial / ecological approaches | Established ways of considering interacting personal, social, and environmental conditions in health and development. | Contextual and structural factors alongside individual experience. | That a framework itself guarantees good practice or outcomes. |
| Social prescribing / human link workers | Personalised connections from people's priorities to community groups and practical support, with human trust and referral paths. | Trusted handoffs, co-produced plans, service-partner relationships, and outcome follow-up. | That every directory listing is current or every intervention works in every context. |
| Human-service directories such as 211 and Findhelp | Existing discovery and referral channels for social supports, often with local operators or coordinated referral workflows. | Do not duplicate them casually: integrate, signpost, or exchange open data where permitted. | Complete coverage, universal availability, or a guarantee that the person successfully received help. |
| Open Referral / HSDS | A machine-readable standard for organizations, services, locations, schedules, service areas, accessibility, and related directory data. | Use a mature interoperability schema instead of inventing a proprietary service catalogue. | That a data record is fresh, accurate, affordable, safe, or a service has capacity today. |

## 7. What to evaluate

Do not benchmark “how integral” a person or community is. Benchmark the tool's performance and real opportunities it helps create.

### Lens-quality tests
- Coverage of relevant individual, relational, cultural, material, institutional, and ecological factors in carefully authored scenarios.
- Provenance accuracy: source text is never conflated with interpretation.
- Contradiction preservation: lenses can disagree without a silent canonical resolution.
- User agency: a lens can be rejected or switched without penalties.
- Non-stigmatization: zero outputs assigning users a spiritual/developmental rank or making access decisions from stage labels.
- Counterfactual consistency: identical substantive evidence receives equivalent evidential scrutiny across identity and worldview cue variants, allowing differences only where context is materially relevant.

### Human-centred validation
Ask people from different traditions, secular users, service navigators, disability-access advocates, frontline workers, and community-led organisations to review the lens prompts. Include people who do not want to discuss spirituality. Compensate community expertise where possible and do not ask people to disclose private experiences to gain access to support.

### Claim ceiling
An accurate lens-selection workflow demonstrates successful perspective support, not an empirically complete model of human development. Any empirical construct or practice still requires its own fit-for-purpose evidence, measurement, and replication.

## 8. Implementation order

1. Publish this lens policy and recruit reviewers across multiple worldviews.
2. Add 8–12 lens-specific adversarial variants to the existing cross-scale synthetic corpus; do not change the frozen v1 corpus in place.
3. Add optional lens selection to Symthaea only after the data contract is accepted; default to a neutral, user-directed frame.
4. Build the city service navigator against Open Referral HSDS and qualified geographic projections. Do not require a theory lens to search for practical support.
5. Use Symtropy only to simulate clearly stated policy/resource hypotheses; use Sol-Atlas for spatial context; keep simulated evidence separate from real-world service outcomes.
6. Evaluate actual task success, not time in app: did the person find a suitable option, understand eligibility/cost/accessibility, make a wanted first contact, and report whether the referral worked?

The governing principle is **pluralistic comprehensiveness without theory capture**: more lenses should increase what can be seen and questioned, not increase the system's power to classify people.
