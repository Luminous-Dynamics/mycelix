# Voluntary Polar Living Routines v0

**Status: S0 design hypotheses only; not a clinical protocol or approved deployment.**

This toolkit asks how Arctic/subarctic and other remote polar communities might voluntarily design routines for rest opportunities, light choices, movement, meals/breaks, social connection, seasonal transitions, safe work handover, environmental comfort, low-risk maintenance prompts and outdoor-readiness reminders.

## Research framing

- [Shao et al. (2024), meta-analysis: Effect of daily light exposure on sleep in polar regions](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.14144) examined 18 studies with 986 participants and found heterogeneous/mixed results; it does not establish a universally effective light regimen.
- [Phase coupling of light exposure and activity differs between Arctic Natives and Non-Natives (2025)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09291016.2025.2507077) reports differences across 63 Arctic residents, a reason to avoid one-size-fits-all assumptions, not a basis for stereotyping or predicting an individual's needs.
- [From Resilient to Thriving: Policy Recommendations to Support Health and Well-being in the Arctic (2022)](https://journalhosting.ucalgary.ca/index.php/arctic/article/view/73953) highlights Indigenous rights/knowledge, community-led research, reciprocity and funding community-based research.
- [BMJ Global Health scoping review (2022) on decolonised Inuit public-health research](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgh-2021-008311) documents gaps in community engagement and Indigenous methods across the reviewed literature.

Evidence supports context-sensitive inquiry—not medical prescriptions or a guarantee of improved health from these routines.

## Non-negotiable design rules

- Every routine is voluntary and adaptable; no universal compliance target.
- No diagnosis, therapy protocol, supplement recommendation or personalized clinical claim.
- No personal or sensitive health data collection by default; no individual adherence score.
- Optional notes are local-only and under the person's control. No health/routine data should be written to a distributed ledger.
- Aggregate evaluation is opt-in, explained in advance and disclosure-reviewed. Small groups can still be identifiable.
- Participation can stop without penalty; null results, adverse outcomes and burdens are reported.
- Community and Indigenous self-determination is required where applicable. Consult and obtain appropriate community governance before a real pilot.
- Formal worker/site safety procedures remain separate and authoritative. This toolkit cannot waive them or assign universal cold/wind exposure limits.

## Evaluation

Co-design a baseline and opt-in pilot with residents/operators before collecting data. Measure acceptability, choice, accessibility, practical barriers, maintenance-report usability and safety-process omissions, with role/season/workload confounders recorded only where proportionate and consented. Prefer aggregate voluntary feedback. Do not treat adherence as productivity or health performance. No wellbeing/clinical outcome is claimed by this proposal.
