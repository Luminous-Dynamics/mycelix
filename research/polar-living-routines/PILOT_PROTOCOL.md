# Polar routine pilot protocol template v0

**Status:** template only. Do not recruit participants or collect personal data before local review, community authorization, appropriate ethics review and explicit opt-in consent.

## 1. Governance before measurement

- Identify the actual community and who has authority to authorize the work; do not presume an external researcher's permission is sufficient.
- Where applicable, engage Indigenous governments/organizations and follow their research, data-sovereignty, language, ownership, access and benefit-sharing requirements.
- Co-design aims, routine options, accessibility accommodations, what counts as harm, interpretation of results, publication and withdrawal procedures.
- Ensure participation is voluntary and refusal/withdrawal has no effect on employment, access to services, housing, benefits or standing in the community.
- The community decides whether any personal data are needed. The default is no personal data collection.

## 2. Research question and preregistration

Before pilot launch, record a dated protocol with:
- primary question and meaningful outcome;
- what would falsify the expected benefit;
- baseline and intervention duration appropriate to local season/work schedule;
- eligibility determined by the community and ethics reviewer, not inferred by a model;
- intended analysis, missing/withdrawn observations, adverse events and uncertainty;
- workload, shift pattern, seasonal light, staffing and infrastructure confounders only to the degree necessary and authorized;
- stop rules and independent contact for concerns.

Do not use routine completion/adherence, sleep timing or self-reported mood as a proxy for employee performance or diagnosis.

## 3. Data minimization and storage

- Prefer anonymous or aggregate voluntary feedback, and use the smallest group size compatible with meaningful non-identifying reporting.
- Never store identifiable health/routine history in public Git, shared issue comments, Mycelix DHT entries or other immutable/distributed storage.
- If a community-approved study needs personal records, use a separately threat-modelled, access-controlled store outside the public receipt/ledger; document controller, access, retention, deletion and breach response before collection.
- Optional personal notes remain local to the participant unless they explicitly choose otherwise.
- Consent is specific, understandable, revocable and recorded through the approved study process. Withdrawing consent stops new collection and triggers the agreed deletion/retention process.

## 4. Candidate outcome measures

Possible measures after local approval:
- perceived usefulness/choice/accessibility;
- participation burden and withdrawal without penalty;
- operational checklist clarity and fault-report resolution time;
- reported barriers and adverse effects;
- safety-procedure omissions/near misses interpreted alongside reporting behavior and exposure;
- optional sleep/wellbeing measures only if ethically approved and voluntarily supplied.

Use qualitative feedback and quantitative measures together. Report null or adverse results; don't rank communities, demographics or individual participants. Do not claim clinical benefit unless a suitable study design and qualified independent review support the specific claim.

## 5. Stop conditions

Pause the study if participants report coercion, privacy or safety incidents, inequitable burden, unacceptable distress, or a community/governance request to stop. Follow the approved escalation plan and retain only the minimum authorized incident record.

## 6. Report

Publish purpose, governance and authorization, method, population/context, participation and withdrawals at a safe aggregate level, outcomes, uncertainty, harms, limitations, conflicts of interest, and what has not been established. Do not publish location-identifying or culturally restricted information without explicit authorization.
