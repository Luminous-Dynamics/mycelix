# PRAXIS-NRES-002: Civic Resilience Learning and Policy-Reasoning Benchmark

**Status:** benchmark proposal and synthetic fixture for review. This is not a validated educational intervention or a finding about which national-service policy is best.  
**Composes:** [PRAXIS-NRES-001](PRAXIS_NRES_001.md), [UNESCO Global Citizenship Education assessment guidance](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/in/rest/annotationSVC/DownloadWatermarkedAttachment/attach_import_59f36c73-b9c9-4898-aea4-f93623d8a898?_=232993eng.pdf), and the [UNDRR Strategic Framework 2026–2030](https://www.undrr.org/strategic-framework-2026-2030).  
**Fixture:** `examples/courses/civic-resilience-policy-benchmark-fixture-001.json`.  
**Learner task:** `examples/courses/civic-resilience-capstone-workbook-001.json`.  
**Scoring anchors:** `PRAXIS_NRES_003_SCORING_ANCHORS.md`.  
**Study protocol:** `PRAXIS_NRES_004_PREREGISTRATION.md`.

## 1. Research question

Does a Praxis course improve durable, transferable competence in preparedness and evidence-based public-policy reasoning compared with a credible existing learning baseline, without increasing false confidence, coercive framing, privacy leakage, or rights misunderstandings?

This is a learning-outcome benchmark, not an enlistment, ideology, political-attitude, or military-performance benchmark. Its policy exercise compares different service models without declaring a universal winner from a synthetic example.

## 2. Paradigm shift

Move from **content completion and quiz scores** to **demonstrated, retained, transferable competence under changing constraints**.

Move from **doctrine as static prose** to **a versioned learning object with explicit sources, decision authorities, assumptions, outcome criteria, counterexamples, correction history, and independent review**.

Move from **one aggregate learner score** to a profile of distinct competencies and non-compensable failure conditions. A high score on cost reasoning cannot compensate for inventing legal authority, ignoring resource infeasibility, fabricating evidence, or grading political conformity.

Move from **an answer-key that chooses a service policy** to **a conditional reasoning benchmark**: learners must state what is known, what is assumed, what remains unknown, what evidence would change their recommendation, and how they would measure the outcome.

No new identity, scoring ledger, authority, or federated-learning protocol is proposed. This fixture sets `fl_enabled=false`; it must not be used to infer enlistment interest, political preferences, ideology, citizenship worth, loyalty, or individual suitability for coercive service.

## 4. Synthetic policy scenario

Fixture `PRAXIS-NRES-002-FIXTURE-001` describes a fictional municipality experiencing a 72-hour power and telecommunications disruption during severe weather. It provides toy resource budgets and service floors. Every numeric value is synthetic and is scoped only to the exercise's immediate response allocation; it must not be misrepresented as a real municipal estimate or a cost/readiness estimate for any service model.

Learners compare four policy options:

1. Universal two-year active military service.
2. Universal preparedness education plus voluntary paid public-resilience pathways.
3. Role-specific professional and reserve pathways.
4. A hybrid model combining education, paid civilian service, and role-specific defence pathways.

For each model, the fixture lists required unknowns: legal pathway, training capacity, resource requirements, retention, educational and employment opportunity costs, equity, accessibility, and how capability would be independently measured. The data deliberately do not assign unsupported benefits, costs, or readiness outcomes to the four models.

The 72-hour recovery plan and the long-term service-policy comparison are distinct questions. Learners must not assume that one short-term response result establishes long-term force effectiveness.

## 5. Scoring rubric and hard gates

The fixture contains eight criterion-referenced dimensions, each scored from 0 to 4:

- evidence discipline;
- legal authority and accountability;
- resource and service feasibility;
- resilience under communication loss;
- equity, accessibility and rights;
- lifecycle and opportunity-cost accounting;
- benchmark quality;
- uncertainty and revisability.

The total is at most 32, but per-dimension results must remain visible. The total is feedback, not a ranking of people and not a political-alignment score.

Hard-fail conditions include fabricating evidence, using unsupported facts as established results, exceeding stated resource limits without flagging infeasibility, treating course or AI outputs as authority, ignoring fundamental rights or due process when proposing compulsion, and scoring learners based on political views or willingness to enlist.

A hard-fail condition is reported separately and cannot be hidden by a high numeric total.

## 6. Adversarial and generalization tests

The fixture defines eight perturbations:

- reduce available budget by 25%;
- lengthen the central-coordination outage;
- reduce confirmed responder availability;
- add accessibility and caregiving constraints;
- inject an unverified persuasive numerical claim;
- introduce contradictory credible cost sources;
- remove evidence about a critical service dependency;
- change the primary service outcome.

A robust learner should revisit the plan and explain the effect of each mutation. A fixed preferred policy that does not change when constraints or objectives materially change is evidence of brittle reasoning, not a successful result.

**Split-integrity requirement:** all eight variants currently committed with the fixture are public development cases, not held-out cases. Never report scores on them as confirmatory generalization evidence. A future benchmark must add a separate pilot/calibration split and genuinely held-out confirmatory cases, with independent custody, preregistered hashes/commitments, exposure logging, a freeze date, and post-analysis disclosure. Expand with held-out fictional regions, hazard combinations, resource distributions, language/accessibility formats, and source-conflict patterns. Course authors and learners must not view confirmatory inputs or scoring keys before the outcome lock.

## 7. Evaluation protocol

### Before the study

1. Define the target learner population, the existing learning baseline, delivery conditions, primary outcome, minimum practically meaningful effect, exclusions and analysis method.
2. Freeze the course and fixture hashes.
3. Freeze rubric anchors and hard-fail examples.
4. Train assessors on separate examples and check rubric reliability before confirmatory scoring.
5. Define privacy/data-retention and accessibility requirements.
6. Preregister the study before exposing confirmatory results.

### During the study

- Use pre-course and post-course assessments.
- Use 30-day and 90-day retention assessments.
- Include a novel, held-out scenario to measure transfer.
- Where feasible and ethical, use random assignment or a staged comparison against a credible existing lesson or instructor-led preparedness course.
- Blind at least two assessors to learner identity and course arm when scoring capstones.
- Do not ask assessors to infer ideology, recruitment propensity, personal loyalty or suitability for military service.
- Separate objective factual scoring from practical skill certification; first-aid certification requires appropriate external practical assessment.

### Report every scheduled outcome

Report means or medians as appropriate, uncertainty intervals, missingness, invalid attempts, drop-out, exclusions, rubric inter-rater agreement, hard-fail frequency, access barriers and fully loaded cost per learner achieving the preregistered competence threshold. Preserve unsuccessful and null results.

A provisional weighted-kappa target of 0.75 is included as a hypothesis for ordinal rubric reliability, not an established field standard. Assessors should verify reliability before it is used as a gate; if prevalence or score distributions make weighted kappa unstable, preregister a suitable alternative agreement statistic before the confirmatory phase.

## 8. Comparison with the state of practice

Use a credible existing preparedness course or instructor-led baseline. The study should measure outcomes the baseline can fairly be expected to teach rather than favouring Praxis because its course covers more topics.

Do not claim superiority from more content, course completion, a single post-test, or synthetic service outcomes alone. The main comparison is whether learners can retain knowledge and correctly apply it to held-out scenarios, handle contradictory evidence, respect legal boundaries, and construct feasible plans at a competitive full cost.

The UNDRR Strategic Framework 2026–2030 emphasizes risk knowledge, locally led disaster risk reduction, financing, and recovery readiness. UNESCO's global citizenship education guidance recommends aligning assessment methods to learning outcomes and using multiple methods rather than relying only on factual recall. The course and benchmark align to those principles; alignment is not certification, endorsement, or proof of effectiveness.

## 9. Provisional acceptance criteria

The first pilot should establish baseline distributions before setting effect-size gates. After that baseline—but before confirmatory runs—pre-register:

- a minimum practically meaningful improvement in a primary learning outcome;
- a retention threshold at 30 or 90 days;
- an inter-rater agreement gate based on pilot reliability;
- a maximum acceptable hard-fail rate, with critical categories investigated individually;
- complete scheduled-run accounting, with each run marked scored, missing with reason, or invalid;
- no unauthorized use of learner data for political or recruitment profiling;
- equal assessment conditions and held-out scenario integrity;
- fully loaded cost per learner reaching the competence threshold.

Any known critical rights/authority/data misuse finding blocks a positive overall result regardless of effect size. A finite synthetic or educational study cannot prove that learners will behave the same way in real emergencies.

## 10. Execution sequence

1. Keep the course and fixture in draft form; verify the JSON structure and links.
2. Have educators, civil-protection practitioners, legal reviewers, accessibility specialists, and learners review the content and rubric.
3. Pilot the workbook and rubric on separate calibration cases; do not reuse pilot cases for confirmatory outcomes. The new workbook, rubric and protocol remain draft-only.
4. Integrate an appropriate curriculum node and generated lesson only after its stable identity and ownership are confirmed.
4. Run the existing read-only curriculum validator on an exact checkout; verify that the generated manifest matches source assets.
5. Pilot assessment reliability and usability using synthetic scenarios.
6. Preregister the confirmatory comparison and held-out variants.
7. Publish exact source/curriculum/fixture digests, methods, aggregate results, limitations, and all null/negative findings.

No current result establishes that the course is active, validated, or better than existing practice. The fixture supplies the start of an evaluable contract, not evidence of success.
